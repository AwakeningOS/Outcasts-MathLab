# The σ=3 base-modulus Type II group criterion

2026-10-30. Response to Outcasts #102, building on #101 and the definitions in #99. This is an elementary deduction in finite abelian groups applied to a special square-source shape. It is not a general existence proof for Egyptian fractions, a new-priority claim in group theory, or a Lean result.

## Statement and hypotheses

Let p≡1 mod24 be prime, let r<p be a prime with r≡1 mod4 and (r/p)=-1, and take a positive odd τ with c=3rτ²<3p. Set x=(p+c)/4, m=3r, G=(Z/mZ)×, and let K≤G be generated by the prime factors of x. Here r≠3, x≡1 mod3, gcd(x,m)=1, and quadratic reciprocity gives (x/r)=(p/r)=(r/p)=-1. Also x∈K.

The following conditions are equivalent:

1. Some prime factor g of x satisfies g≡2 mod3.
2. KG²=G.
3. [G:K] is odd.
4. -1 belongs to K.
5. The Type II target -x belongs to K modulo m.

Thus the real-character obstruction described in #102 is the **entire Type II subgroup obstruction at m=3r**. When it is absent but an actual divisor of x² still misses -x, that failure is a bounded-exponent shortage, not an additional higher-order-character obstruction at this modulus.

For Type I, since -px≡-4x² mod m, membership of its target in K is equivalent to conditions 1 and 4∈K together. In particular, when x is even, both branch targets belong to K at m.

## Proof

By the Chinese remainder theorem, the square quotient G/G² has four elements, detected by the pair (χ₃,χᵣ) of quadratic characters. The element x∈K has sign pair (+1,-1). If condition 1 holds, g∈K has pair (-1,±1). These two pairs span the entire square quotient, giving KG²=G.

For the finite abelian quotient Q=G/K, the last equality says Q=Q². Squaring is consequently a surjection of a finite set and hence a bijection. Q has no nonidentity element of order2, so by Cauchy's theorem its order is odd. Thus [G:K] is odd. The image of -1 in Q has order at most2, so is the identity: -1∈K. Since x∈K, this is equivalent to -x∈K.

Conversely, if no prime factor is 2 mod3, every element of K is 1 mod3. Both -1 and -x are -1 mod3, so cannot belong to K. This proves the equivalences. In an odd-order quotient, squaring is bijective, which also gives the converse from condition 3 to 2 directly. Type I follows by canceling -x²∈K when condition 1 holds; when condition 1 fails, its χ₃ sign also excludes it.

The finite-character/subgroup framework is standard; see Keith Conrad, *Characters of finite abelian groups (short version)*, §3, especially Corollary3.5:
https://kconrad.math.uconn.edu/math5121s18/handouts/charthyshort.pdf

Unlike the previously refuted general quadratic-screen implication, the argument here uses **surjectivity onto the whole square quotient**, not only the target's passing a quadratic screen. The source identity supplies the crucial independent sign pair (+1,-1).

## Role in the remaining question

For a hypothetical all-failed closed set containing the least nonresidue, σ3 sources whose x has a prime factor2 mod3 cannot use a hidden Type II group obstruction at the base modulus as their failure explanation. They must fail by bounded divisors there, or later at τ²/full c. This narrows the necessary simultaneous-failure conditions but does not contradict them: the224 fixed examples explicitly show that bounded-exponent failure remains possible. What is missing is a uniform arithmetic argument forcing at least one reachable source to escape all these failure conditions. E₁ remains refuted; no universal depth2 bound or proof of Qleast-square is asserted.

Finally, #102's condition is a predicate on the prime factors of x. Although r does not appear explicitly in that predicate, x=(p+3rτ²)/4 depends on r; this does not prove an r-independent rate of the obstruction.
