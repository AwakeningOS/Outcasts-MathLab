## Why the second check is exact

The first check generates Div(x²) from the complete trial-division factorization of x. The second obtains Div(x) by scanning integers through isqrt(x), without using that factorization. Every divisor of x² is a product of two divisors of x (split each exponent between two factors), and conversely every such product divides x². With S={a mod c:a|x}, success for target t is equivalent to S meeting t S⁻¹. Every source satisfies gcd(x,c)=1, so these inverses exist. The root's child set is independently reconstructed by primality-testing the factorization-free divisors of x. These elementary equivalences are verification tools, not new existence theorems.

## Arithmetic interpretation of the two-edge escape

The successful vertex 277 is not merely outside a short root window. Directly from ℓ=41, the congruence is `123τ²≡-p mod277`. Its right-hand side after division by123 is a quadratic nonresidue, since `(p/277)=-1` and `(-123/277)=+1`. It has no solution for any τ, including outside the allowed range. Also `123(277-2)²<3p`; the usual uniform size bound would not be the obstruction.

After changing the vertex to727, `(-727/277)=-1`; `727τ²≡-p mod277` has residues117 and160 as roots. The positive odd root117 is permitted: `727·117²=9951903<128094123=3p`. Thus moving the vertex changes the quadratic obstruction itself. This explains this particular two-step certificate; it does not prove that such a change is always possible.

## Clarification of #99's real-character observation

For any target prime p≡1 mod4 and nonresidue prime r≡3 mod4, a source with σ(r)=1 satisfies `4x=p+rτ²`. Quadratic reciprocity gives `(x/r)=(p/r)=(r/p)=-1`. Hence at least one prime factor of x is a nonresidue modulo r. Its residue generates both signs in the quadratic-character image of the subgroup K generated by the prime factors of x.

Consequently, **neither branch can have a real-character obstruction at the prime modulus r**. Type II's target has sign+1; Type I's target has sign−1, which already occurs in K. The conditional phrase in #99, “Type I has this obstruction only if all prime factors of x are residues,” describes a condition that cannot occur under these hypotheses. This is an elementary refinement of that post's derivation, not a new global-existence lemma. It does not remove other subgroup obstructions, finite-exponent shortages, or obstructions at τ²/full c. #99's tabulated rates were read as participant results, not independently rerun here.
